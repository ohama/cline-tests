<objective>
Have a human ratify the keep-or-revert decision, apply it, and close the phase with an honest
account of itself.

Purpose: USE-03's deliverable is the recorded decision, and this is where it becomes real. It gets a
human because it changes the default behaviour of a tool the user will actually type — if the
decision is revert, `cline-plan` stops requesting reasoning at all, and that should not happen
silently on an agent's judgement. The mechanical rule's output is already computed and
pre-registered; the human's role is to ratify it, or to override it with a stated reason that goes
on the record next to the rule's output.

Output: the `DECISION:` line in `phase-11/AB-RESULTS.md`, a possibly-changed `phase-11/wrapper.env`,
and `phase-11/PHASE-11-FINDINGS.md`.

**Outcome neutrality:** "revert to `flashnext`" is a full, successful completion of USE-03 and of
ROADMAP criterion 3, not a failed phase. So is "keep". So is "the A/B could not discriminate, and
the default is left as-is / reverted for the stated reason". The requirement is the recorded
judgement with its evidence, and the phase is complete when that exists — never when a particular
direction was found.
</objective>

  <decision>Should `cline-plan` keep targeting `flashnext-plan`, or revert to `flashnext`?</decision>
  <context>
Present, from `phase-11/AB-RESULTS.md`, without editorialising:
- §4's per-arm four-category aggregate (correct / incorrect / no-answer / no-output) and the median

  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="ratify-rule-output">
      <name>Ratify the pre-registered rule's output</name>
      <pros>The rule was fixed before the data; ratifying it keeps the decision unfitted to the
        result, which is the whole reason it was pre-registered.</pros>
      <cons>None specific to either direction — this is the default path.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="override-with-reason">
      <name>Override the rule's output, with a stated reason</name>
      <pros>Legitimate when the run surfaced something the rule could not anticipate — e.g. a large
        latency penalty alongside a marginal accuracy gain, or a truncation rate that makes the
        thinking arm unusable in practice regardless of accuracy.</pros>
      <cons>The override and its reason must be recorded next to the rule's output in
        `AB-RESULTS.md`, and the findings must disclose that the shipped default does not follow the
        pre-registered rule.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="defer-insufficient-evidence">
      <name>Declare the evidence insufficient and set the default on the stated conservative
        ground</name>
      <pros>Honest when a void condition triggered or the run was too small to resolve anything; the
        default is then set by policy (USE-03's own "no improvement → revert") rather than by a
        number that does not support a conclusion.</pros>
      <cons>USE-03 closes on weaker evidence, and the findings must say so and name what a future
        phase would need to measure to close it properly.</cons>
    </option>
  </options>
  <resume-signal>Reply with `ratify-rule-output`, `override-with-reason` (and the reason), or
    `defer-insufficient-evidence` (and which default to set). The reply's exact wording is recorded
    verbatim in `AB-RESULTS.md` §11.</resume-signal>
