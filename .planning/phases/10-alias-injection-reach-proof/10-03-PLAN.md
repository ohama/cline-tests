<objective>
🔴 **This is the maintenance window. This plan, and only this plan, may restart
`com.ohama.litellm`.** It is also the only plan in this phase permitted to write
`/Users/ohama/agent-stack/litellm/config.yaml`.

Purpose: install the three new aliases into the file the running gateway actually loads, and get
the gateway back up, with every step gated by something that has already been proven to fail when it
should. The sequence is deliberately front-loaded with the cheap failures: build and break the
safety envelope; restart on the *unmodified* config to prove the restart mechanism works and to
turn the downtime estimate into a measurement; re-run the validation ladder; only then write the
live file.

Restarting first on the pristine config costs one extra short outage and buys the ability to tell
two very different failures apart. If litellm does not come back after Restart B, we already know
whether the restart mechanism was healthy ten minutes earlier — so the fault is the config, and the
rollback is the right response. Without Restart A, a failed Restart B is ambiguous and the operator
is debugging under an outage.

Waves in this phase are strictly serial, and this is why: nothing may measure the stack while the
stack is being replaced, and the model runs `--max-num-seqs 1` so no two plans may issue requests
concurrently.

Output: the mutated live config, `phase-10/probe_lib10.sh` and its self-test,
`phase-10/apply_candidate.sh`, `phase-10/health_check.sh`, `phase-10/MAINTENANCE-LOG.md`,
`phase-10/CFG-13-EVIDENCE.md`.

**Outcome neutrality:** a window that ends in a verified rollback is a correctly-executed window,
not a failed plan. What would fail this plan is installing a config without re-validating it,
declaring health from a single sample, or leaving the stack in a state nobody measured.
</objective>
